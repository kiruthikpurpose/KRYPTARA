# Global Opportunity Intelligence Report — November 2026
## Where the next category-defining companies will be built (2–5 year entry, 5–15 year foresight)

The most important finding is that the biggest near-term venture opportunity is not AI models. It is the physical bottlenecks that AI and electrification have exposed: grid capacity, long-lead electrical equipment, thermal management, and ageing infrastructure. The best entry points are capital-light "intelligence + service" businesses that help scarce existing assets connect faster, carry more and last longer. Our two top-ranked opportunities are large-load grid-capacity intelligence (84/100) and transformer health and life-extension (83/100). Both rest on independently measured scarcity: US power transformer lead times averaged 128 weeks in Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 survey, and the median US project built in 2025 took more than 5 years from interconnection request to commercial operation (Berkeley Lab, Queued Up 2026).\[1\]\[2\]

## TL;DR

- **The AI economy is constrained by physical infrastructure, not software.** The IEA's *Energy and AI* report (Apr 2025) projects data-centre electricity use will more than double, from around 415 TWh (about 1.5% of global electricity in 2024) to around 945 TWh (just under 3%) by 2030. Wood Mackenzie expected a 30% supply deficit for US power transformers in 2025, and interconnection takes 5+ years. The strongest founder bets help scarce grid assets connect faster, carry more and last longer: capacity intelligence, transformer health, a certified secondary equipment market, and liquid-cooling integrity.
- **Fixed regulatory deadlines create budgets; slipping ones create risk.** Fixed demand comes from EU battery passports (18 Feb 2027), CNSA 2.0 acquisition rules (2027) and NIST's deprecation of RSA/ECC after 2030. Other rules have slipped: the US PHMSA leak-detection rule (withdrawn, still "TBD"), the EU methane importer MRV (the Commission proposed a delay to 2029 on 14 Oct 2026)\[3\] and US PFAS limits (partly rescinded, deadline moving toward 2031). Treat those as upside, not the core thesis.
- **"Boring" contrarian markets offer better risk-adjusted returns than headline frontier technology.** Liemberger & Wyatt (*Water Supply*, 2019) estimate non-revenue water at 126 bn m³/yr, worth US$39bn/yr even at a conservative US$0.31/m³. The NACE IMPACT study (CORROSION 2016) puts the cost of corrosion at US$2.5T, or 3.4% of 2013 global GDP, with US$375–875bn/yr savable. Inspection-to-maintenance workflows and controlled-mineral compliance are similar. Humanoids, quantum navigation and fusion are real but mostly early. What is investable today is the infrastructure that supports them, not the headline device.

# VOLUME I — METHOD AND MACRO SCAN

## Methodology
We worked market-first:
1. **Macro scan.** Verified each shift against 2024–2026 sources: IEA, Berkeley Lab (LBNL), Wood Mackenzie, BNEF, Stanford AI Index, NIST, European Commission, EPA, PHMSA, WHO, ASCE, Deloitte/Manufacturing Institute and IATA.
2. **Systems tracing.** Traced each shift to the new bottlenecks it creates.
3. **Whitespace.** Separated gaps in existing markets from genuinely new categories.
4. **Hypotheses.** Generated 50 opportunity hypotheses.
5. **Validation.** Challenged each on buyer, budget, competition, regulatory dependency and capital intensity.
6. **Scoring.** Applied the 100-point model.

Opportunities resting on soft or reversible regulation were penalised. Market size never compensated for missing demand. Labels: *Verified*, *Estimate* (our bottom-up with assumptions), *Hypothesis*. Third-party market-size figures are flagged where they conflict. For example, 2026 data-centre liquid-cooling market estimates range from US$4.07bn (MarketsandMarkets) to ~US$6bn+ (others), so we do not rely on them.\[4\]\[5\]

## Deliverable 5 — Top 20 Dossiers

Score key: Market/15, Pain/15, WTP/15, Timing/10, Growth/10, Whitespace/10, Feasibility/10, Moat/5, Capital efficiency/5, Expansion/5.

### 1. Large-load grid-capacity & interconnection intelligence — 84/100
1. **Name:** "Where's the power?" time-to-power intelligence.
2. **Classification:** Energy / transmission & interconnection; software; power-flow modelling.
3. **Thesis:** Power availability is now the first siting question for data centres, fabs and electrified industry. Queue data is public but fragmented, and hosting capacity is opaque. Bankable time-to-power estimates sell into multi-billion-dollar siting decisions.
4. **Shift:** Data-centre demand doubles by 2030 (IEA). The US queue holds 1,312 GW of generation and 749 GW of storage, with a median of >5 years to build and 13% completion of 2000–2020 requests (LBNL 2026).\[6\]\[7\]
5. **Problem:** Developers option land, then learn 12–24 months later from a utility study that capacity is years away. Idle capital burns every month.
6. **Existing solutions:** Utility pre-application meetings, consultants, and queue/site tools (Grid Status, Interconnection.fyi, Paces, LandGate, Enverus). Most show queues, not load-side time-to-power that includes equipment lead times.
7. **Market (Estimate):** 2,000–4,000 serious developers, utilities and investors × US$100–400k/yr ≈ US$0.4–1.6bn. Confidence medium.
8. **Product:** Ingest queues, ISO filings, substation maps and lead-time indices. Run a DC power-flow screen. Output probabilistic time-to-power with confidence intervals and flexible-connection scenarios.
9. **First customer:** Site-selection teams at data-centre developers and neoclouds. The budget owner is the head of development.
10. **Business model:** Annual SaaS plus per-site studies; later, success fees.
11. **Competition:** The tools above (direct), engineering consultancies (indirect), utilities (partners).
12. **Timing:** Queued gas rose 86% in 2025, and large-load tariffs are emerging.\[7\]
13. **Feasibility:** Public data plus open-source power flow. Data quality outside ISOs is the main risk.
14. **Entry:** Paid PJM/ERCOT pilot with 5 developers.
15. **Expansion:** UK/EU (UK: 140 schemes, ~50 GW), flexible-load contracting, capacity marketplace.\[37\]
16. **Risks:** Utilities publish their own maps; forecast misses erode trust; data vendors bundle the feature.
17. **Score:** 11/14/13/9/10/6/9/3/5/4. Confidence High. Commercial, early growth, low capital, medium sales cycle. First customer in 3–6 months; product-market fit in 12–18 months.
18. **Sources:** IEA *Energy and AI* (Apr 2025); LBNL *Queued Up 2026* (Jun 2026); Wood Mackenzie (Sep 2025).

**Pattern:** intelligence layer → platform. **Moat:** a track record of forecast accuracy. **What would disprove it:** utilities' own maps prove accurate, or developers won't pay >$100k/yr.

### 2. Transformer health & life-extension — 83/100
1. **Name:** Transformer life-extension-as-a-service.
2. **Classification:** Energy / substations; hybrid.
3. **Thesis:** With replacement units ~2.5 years away, every existing transformer is a strategic asset.\[39\] Sensors plus service that predict failure, extend life and guarantee availability capture part of the avoided outage cost.
4. **Shift:** Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 survey put lead times at 128 weeks for power transformers and 144 weeks for GSUs. It expected a 30% power-transformer supply deficit in 2025, and its analyst Ben Boucher estimated imports at ~80% of 2025 US power-transformer supply. EU+NA demand of 914 GVA against 566 GVA supply (McKinsey).
5. **Problem:** Oil is sampled annually or less. Mid-size industrial and data-centre units are often unmonitored. One failure can stall a plant for years.
6. **Existing solutions:** OEMs (Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, GE Vernova) and instrumentation firms (Qualitrol, Doble, Camlin) focus on large transmission units. The gap is the long tail plus outcome-based service.
7. **Market (Estimate):** ~500k medium/large units (assumption, low confidence) × 20% × US$3–8k/yr ≈ US$0.3–0.8bn recurring, plus refurbishment.
8. **Product:** Retrofit DGA, thermal and partial-discharge sensing; cellular gateway; health index and remaining-life model; oil-regeneration partner dispatch.
9. **First customer:** Critical-facilities leads at data centres and industrial plants.
10. **Business model:** Hardware-as-a-service, plus availability-guarantee tiers backed by insurance.
11. **Competition:** OEMs (direct at utilities), test service firms (indirect), insurers and regen contractors (partners). Differentiation: vendor-neutral coverage of mixed fleets.
12. **Timing:** Deficits persist toward 2030.\[39\]\[40\]
13. **Feasibility:** Mature sensing. Validating the life model is the hard part.
14. **Entry:** 50-unit pilot across 3 campuses at ~US$5k/unit/yr.
15. **Expansion:** Switchgear, GSUs, underwriting data, secondary-market certification (Dossier 6).
16. **Risks:** OEM bundling; slow utility procurement; urgency fades after 2030.
17. **Score:** 12/14/13/9/9/6/9/3/4/4. Confidence High on pain, medium on sizing. Commercial, medium capital. First revenue in 6–9 months.
18. **Sources:** Wood Mackenzie (Aug/Sep 2025); McKinsey (2026); ASCE 2025.

**Pattern:** new-bottleneck business plus intelligence layer. **What would disprove it:** failure rates too low to justify monitoring spend.

### 3. Liquid-cooling integrity platform — 81/100
1. **Name:** Coolant integrity, leak detection and commissioning assurance.
2. **Classification:** AI infrastructure / thermal; hybrid.
3. **Thesis:** Racks run at 70–140 kW and are heading to ~600 kW. Fluid drift, micro-leaks and poor commissioning are now single points of failure next to millions of dollars of GPUs, and nobody owns them end to end.
4. **Shift:** JLL says >70 kW requires liquid cooling. Fairwater runs 140 kW per rack; Kyber targets ~600 kW.\[9\]\[10\]
5. **Problem:** Air-cooling-trained teams rely on manual coolant sampling. Leaks are found late. Warranty disputes span the OEM, CDU vendor and colo.
6. **Existing solutions:** CDU vendors (Vertiv, Schneider Electric, CoolIT, Motivair) offer basic telemetry; water-treatment firms sample periodically. Nobody provides a vendor-neutral, warranty-grade record.
7. **Market (Estimate):** 5,000–10,000 liquid-cooled halls by 2030 (assumption) × US$50–150k/yr ≈ US$0.25–1.5bn.
8. **Product:** Inline conductivity/pH/inhibitor/particulate sensors, cable leak detection, anomaly model, and a commissioning app producing a digital record.
9. **First customer:** VP of operations at neoclouds and colos retrofitting for GPUs.
